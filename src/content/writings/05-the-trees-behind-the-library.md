There’s a shortcut behind the library that I take most days. It’s also the route I walk on Fridays to pray Jumaat. There are trees along the path and for some reason, I’ve gotten into the habit of taking photos of them.

I didn't plan to document anything but one day I noticed the leaves had started turning yellow. Some had already fallen to the ground but there was something about the contrast of the colours that caught my attention. I stopped, took a picture and continued walking.

I was scrolling through the photos on my phone and noticed something.

The first photo I took was from October last year.

![Beautiful tree with yellow leaves](/20251014_133226.jpg "Beautiful tree with yellow leaves") <p style="text-align: center;"><em>Taken by me on October 14, 2025</em></p>

The second was from January this year. Bare branches against the sky.

![Leaves gone, branches bare, lonely in the cold](/20260119_103723.jpg "Leaves gone, branches bare, lonely in the cold") <p style="text-align: center;"><em>Taken by me on January 19, 2026</em></p>

The third was from last week. Green again. Full again.

![All green again, full of life](/20260528_155912.jpg "All green again, full of life") <p style="text-align: center;"><em>Taken by me on May 28, 2026</em></p>

Same trees. Same spot.

I hadn't realized it at the time but those photos had quietly recorded a cycle.

*October.*

*January.*

*May.*

A while ago, I came across an idea on someone's WhatsApp status.

The shape of it was simple.

> You can't learn the skill because you can't afford the tuition. You can't start the thing because you've never had the exposure that would make it possible. People can't trust you with it because they've never seen you do it. Each lack quietly guards the door to the thing that would end it.

I remember reading it and immediately understanding what it meant.

Lately, I've found myself thinking about that idea.

Not because I was lacking ability. The ability was still there. What changed was the environment around it.

There are situations where what you know how to do and what you are allowed to do become two different things. A person can stay exactly the same while the opportunities available to them change completely.

I think that's what I found difficult to understand at first.

For a long time, I thought stability was something you built for yourself. Now I think a lot of what feels like stability comes from familiarity. Being in a place where people know your history. A place where you've already earned trust. A place where the path between your abilities and opportunities is short.

Move somewhere new and some of that resets.

Not because you've become less capable.

The new place simply doesn't know you yet.

Around the same time I was thinking about all this, I found myself looking at those tree photos again.

The January picture is the one that held my attention.

If you had only seen the tree in January, you might have thought that was its final form. Bare branches. No leaves. No visible sign that anything was happening.

But looking at the three photos together, I noticed something else.

In the first photo, the yellow tree is the first thing you see. There's a smaller evergreen beside it but you barely notice it. The larger tree dominates the picture.

In January, that changes completely.

The evergreen suddenly becomes the most alive thing in the frame. The tree that stood out in October fades into the background. If you didn't know they were part of the same cycle, you might assume one was thriving while the other was struggling.

Then May arrives and the balance shifts again.

The larger tree is full of leaves. Green everywhere. The evergreen is still there, looking much the same as it did before but your attention returns to the tree that seemed finished a few months earlier.

It made me wonder how often we judge things in the middle of a season.

Some winters are longer than others. Some things don't return in the form we expect. Not every difficult season ends exactly the way we hope.

But seasons do change.

A difficult period can feel permanent while you're inside it. It can start to look like a description of who you are rather than where you are.

Then enough time passes and you realize you were measuring yourself in the middle of a season.

A tree can look diminished simply because it is being measured at the wrong time.
